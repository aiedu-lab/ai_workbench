# ⚡ Speed Reading

## 🤔 Why This Method

- 😩 Traditional learning methods can be painful and slow.
- 📉 Brute force reading is tedious and has a low ROI.
- 🚀 Layered reading and ad-hoc searching is not just more efficient but more enjoyable.

## 🏛️ The 6 Pillars

1. 📊 Pareto's Principle
2. 🧅 Layered Reading
3. 🔍 Inquiry-Based Learning
4. 🎛️ Order Control
5. 🔄 Multi-Pass Reading
6. 🗺️ Visual Notes

### 📊 1. Pareto's Principle

- 📚 Books are too long, filled with fluff.
- 💎 The gist or value can be found in 20% of the book.
- ⚖️ ~80% of consequences come from 20% of the causes.
- 🎯 Focus effort on the high-value 20%.

### 🧅 2. Layered Reading

Layered reading surfaces anchors and concepts progressively. The definition of
layers vary by reading material. One possibility might be:

- 🌐 Layer 1 is the Table of Contents.
- 📖 Layer 2 is the first and last paragraphs of each chapter.
- 🔎 Layer 3 might be the first and last paragraphs of each section within
  chapters.
- 🔬 Layer 4 might the first and last paragraph of each subsection with chapters,
  as well as the first and last sentence of each page.

Descend deeper as needed; adapt to the book's structure.

### 🔍 3. Inquiry-Based Learning

- ❓ Reading while looking for answers to questions you have is way more efficient
  than reading passively.
- 💡 Inquiry-Based Learning converts reading from passive to active. Your brain is
  far more engaged that way.

### 🎛️ 4. Order Control

- 🚫📋 No need to go through the material sequentially.
- 🧠 Read in the order your brain wants to read.
- 🦅 Survey the material at a bird's eye view — skim first. Then descend based
  on interest or the desire to understand more.
- 🕹️ You are in control of what to explore deeply and what to skip.

### 🔄 5. Multi-Pass Reading

- 🧀 The brain is like Swiss cheese — learning is best absorbed in layers.
- 🧠 Multi-pass reading aids retention by revisiting material at increasing depth.
- 🕳️ Each pass fills in gaps from the previous one.

### 🗺️ 6. Visual Notes: MindMaps

MindMaps help organize and retain information visually.

**✨ Key principles:** These are derived from Justin Sung's
[GRINDE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Grd7K7bJVWg) maps.

- 🔢 Group no more than **5** items per node
- 🪞 Reflective — captures your understanding
- 🔗 Interconnected — shows relationships without making edges cross each other
- 🖼️ Non-verbal where possible
- ➡️ Directionality to show the flow or causality
- 🌟 Emphasize the nodes and edges to highlight importance
- 🃏 Very detailed information should be put in flash cards or bulleted notes —
  not in the MindMap.

**🏗️ Layering/hierarchy order:** use numbered levels (1 → 2 → 3 → ...) to guide depth of detail.

⚓ From each layer, extract some key phrases (aka anchors). And then add these
anchors to the MindMap. Thus, the MindMap is built progressively from layer 1
down to the lower layers. Use the following colors in the MindMap:

- ⬛ Black: Layer 1 anchors and edges
- 💚 Green: Layer 2 anchors and edges
- 🟠 Orange: Layer 3 anchors and edges
- 🔴 Red: Layer 4 anchors and edges
- 🔵 Blue: Layer 5 anchors and edges
- 🟣 Purple: Layer 6 anchors and edges

## 🔧 Strategy: putting everything together

- 📄 Flip through the pages of the reading material, and prescriptively define
  what constitutes each layer.
- 🎯 Read for only two reasons — either to extract anchors from the layers, or
  to answer questions. When extracting anchors, it is only permitted to read
  the prescriptive definitions of layers. When answering questions, it is
  permitted to read anything.
- 🔽 Starting with layer 1, iteratively descend within each layer.
  - ⚓ Extract anchors from the current layer.
  - 🗺️ When sufficient amount of that layer has been read (e.g., within a
    chapter), put the extracted anchors into the MindMap. For each node, write
    both what it is and why it matters. Read to answer any questions that might
    arise. Detailed notes if any should be put in flash cards or bulleted notes
    — not in the MindMap.
  - Either hand draw the MindMap, or have an AI coding assistant build the
    MindMap in an HTML file.
  - ✂️ Periodically prune the map. Remove nodes that are interesting but not
    important, redundant with their parent, or too low-value to justify screen
    space and attention.
  - ➡️ Continue either reading the rest of that layer (e.g., from the next
    chapter), or descend to the next layer within whatever part of the
    current layer that has already been read.
