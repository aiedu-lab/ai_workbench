# Hans Moravec (born 1948)

Austrian-American roboticist and futurist at Carnegie Mellon.
Argued during the first [[AI Winter]] that hardware — not algorithms
— was the bottleneck preventing AI.

## The Moravec Compute Estimate (1976)
During the [[AI Winter]], Moravec measured the compute gap precisely:
computers in 1976 were "millions of times too weak" to exhibit
intelligence. He used an analogy:

> "Artificial intelligence requires computer power in the same way
> that aircraft require horsepower. Below a certain threshold, it's
> impossible, but, as power increases, eventually it could become easy.
> With enough horsepower, anything will fly."

This framing — intelligence as a hardware threshold problem — was
prescient. [[Moore's Law]] delivered the doublings Moravec said were
needed; [[Deep Learning]] crossed the threshold with [[AlexNet]]
in 2012.

## Moravec's Paradox
Separately, Moravec observed (1988) that tasks easy for humans
(perception, mobility) are hard for AI, while tasks hard for humans
(chess, theorem proving) are easy for AI. This paradox was later
partially resolved by [[Deep Learning]], which handles perception
through hardware-scale training rather than hand-coded rules.
