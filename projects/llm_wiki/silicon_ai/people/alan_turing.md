# Alan Turing (1912–1954)

British mathematician and computer scientist. His theoretical
work established the foundations of computer science and AI.

## Key Contributions
- **Turing Machine (1936):** An abstract model showing any
  computable function can be mechanically executed — the
  theoretical basis for programmable computers and the Church-
  Turing thesis. Sets the stage for all AI reasoning.
- **Turing Test (1950):** "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"
  proposed testing machine intelligence by conversational
  indistinguishability from a human. Still referenced in
  [[Large Language Models]] evaluation.
- **Codebreaking (WWII):** Helped break the Enigma cipher using
  Bombe machines — an early demonstration that computation
  can solve intelligent tasks.
- **Reinforcement Learning precursor:** Turing and [[Arthur
  Samuel]] foresaw the role of reward-based learning in AI.

## Hardware Vision
The Ferranti Mark 1 (1951) ran the first chess program —
proving hardware-enabled AI. [[Deep Blue]]'s 1997 chess victory
used a machine 10 million times faster, illustrating how
[[Moore's Law]] progressively realized Turing's vision.

## Legacy at Dartmouth
Turing's computational theory inspired [[John McCarthy]] and
[[Marvin Minsky]] to found AI as a formal discipline at the
1956 Dartmouth Workshop.
