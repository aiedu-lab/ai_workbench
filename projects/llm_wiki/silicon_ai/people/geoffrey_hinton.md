# Geoffrey Hinton (b. 1947)

British-Canadian computer scientist, called the "Godfather of
Deep Learning." Key pioneer of [[Neural Networks]] and the
[[Deep Learning]] revolution.

## Key Contributions
- **Boltzmann Machine (1983):** Proved, with John Hopfield,
  that certain [[Neural Networks]] can converge and learn —
  reversing earlier assumptions that nonlinear nets behave
  chaotically.
- **Backpropagation (1986):** Popularized with David Rumelhart;
  enabled training multi-layer [[Neural Networks]] through
  gradient descent. The training algorithm underlying all
  [[Deep Learning]].
- **AlexNet (2012):** Student Alex Krizhevsky, supervised by
  Hinton, trained [[AlexNet]] on two consumer GPUs — the
  breakthrough that launched the [[Deep Learning]] era.
- **Google acquisition (2012):** Sold his lab to Google for $44M
  at a Lake Tahoe AI auction; Baidu had made the initial bid.
- **2024 Nobel Prize in Physics:** Shared with John Hopfield for
  foundational contributions to machine learning.

## The Hardware-AI Timeline
Hinton's career arc illustrates the silicon-AI link directly:
his 1986 [[Backpropagation]] algorithm was correct but
impractical. He acknowledged: "our labeled datasets were
thousands of times too small and our computers were millions
of times too slow."

[[Moore's Law]] delivered the needed hardware in two stages:
1. CPU scaling enabled basic machine learning through the 1990s
2. GPU parallel compute (post [[Dennard Scaling]] end) enabled
   billion-parameter [[Deep Learning]] by 2012
