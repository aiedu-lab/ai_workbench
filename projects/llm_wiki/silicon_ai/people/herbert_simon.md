# Herbert Simon (1916–2001)

Computer scientist, cognitive psychologist, and Nobel laureate.
Co-creator of the [[Logic Theorist]] (1956) and the General Problem
Solver with [[Allen Newell]] — founding works of [[Symbolic AI]].

## Contributions
- **[[Logic Theorist]] (1955–56):** Co-created with [[Allen Newell]]
  and J. C. Shaw. Proved 38 of 52 theorems in Russell and Whitehead's
  *Principia Mathematica* — first demonstration of machine reasoning
  beyond arithmetic.
- **General Problem Solver (1957):** Generalized the Logic Theorist
  into a universal problem-solving architecture. Foundational to
  [[Symbolic AI]] planning research.
- **Bounded rationality:** Economic theory that decision-makers work
  with limited information and cognitive resources — connecting AI
  and economic modeling.
- **Nobel Prize in Economics (1978):** Awarded for bounded rationality
  research — the only AI founder to receive a Nobel.

## Famous Claim
After the Logic Theorist, Simon declared:

> "We have invented a computer program capable of thinking
> non-numerically, and thereby solved the venerable mind/body
> problem, explaining how a system composed of matter can have
> the properties of mind."

A claim that proved premature — the limits of [[Symbolic AI]] and
the emergence of [[Machine Learning]] would not be fully understood
for decades.

## Hardware Awareness
Simon and Newell understood that hardware constrained AI progress.
They designed the Logic Theorist for JOHNNIAC (~5 MHz equivalent).
Simon predicted in 1957 that within 10 years a computer would be
world chess champion — off by 40 years, largely due to hardware
timelines ([[Deep Blue]] won in 1997).
