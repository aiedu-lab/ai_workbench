# Symbolic AI

An approach to AI that represents intelligence through explicit
symbols and logical rules — programs that manipulate symbols
according to formal logic. Also called "Good Old-Fashioned AI"
(GOFAI) or the "neat" paradigm.

## Origins
Founded at the 1956 Dartmouth Workshop by [[John McCarthy]],
[[Marvin Minsky]], [[Herbert Simon]], and [[Allen Newell]].
The first AI program, the [[Logic Theorist]] (Newell & Simon,
1955), proved theorems by symbolic manipulation.

## Key Systems
- **Logic Theorist (1955):** Proved 38/52 theorems from
  *Principia Mathematica*
- **General Problem Solver (1957):** Captured a general
  search-based problem-solving algorithm
- **Expert Systems (1980s):** MYCIN (medical diagnosis),
  R1 (equipment configuration); commercially successful
  until the Second [[AI Winter]]
- **Prolog (1972):** Logic programming language enabling
  tractable deduction

## Hardware Assumption
Symbolic AI researchers assumed the hardware bottleneck was
temporary — [[Moore's Law]] would deliver sufficient compute.
This was correct, but the combinatorial explosion problem
meant symbolic approaches scaled poorly regardless of hardware.

## Contrast with Connectionism
[[Neural Networks]] (the "scruffy" approach) learn from data
without explicit rules. [[Marvin Minsky]] championed frames;
[[Geoffrey Hinton]] called symbolic logic the "luminous aether
of AI." The [[Deep Learning]] revolution of 2012 ([[AlexNet]])
settled the debate — data-driven learning outperformed
hand-crafted symbolic rules for perception tasks.

## Legacy
Object-oriented programming inherits Minsky's "frames."
Logic programming and knowledge representation remain active
in specialized domains. Hybrid neuro-symbolic approaches are
an active research area.
