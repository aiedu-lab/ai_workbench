# Logic Theorist (1955–1956)

The first AI program. Created by [[Allen Newell]] and
[[Herbert Simon]] with help from J. C. Shaw. Presented at the
1956 Dartmouth Workshop — the founding event of AI as a
discipline.

## What It Did
The Logic Theorist proved 38 of the first 52 theorems in
Russell and Whitehead's *Principia Mathematica* — and found
new, more elegant proofs for some. It demonstrated that a
digital computer could perform symbolic reasoning, not just
arithmetic.

## Significance
- First demonstration of machine intelligence beyond arithmetic.
- Launched the [[Symbolic AI]] paradigm that dominated AI
  research and funding from 1956 to the mid-1990s.
- Simon declared they had "solved the venerable mind/body
  problem, explaining how a system composed of matter can
  have the properties of mind."

## Hardware Context
Ran on the JOHNNIAC computer at RAND Corporation.
The JOHNNIAC had ~5 MHz equivalent performance. By contrast,
[[AlexNet]] (2012) ran on [[GPU Computing|GPUs]] with trillions
of floating-point operations per second — illustrating 57 years
of [[Moore's Law]] scaling separating the two systems.
