# Integrated Circuit (IC)

An electronic circuit where all components (transistors,
resistors, capacitors) are fabricated on a single semiconductor
substrate. The IC is the physical substrate of [[Moore's Law]].

## Invention
- **1958:** Jack Kilby (Texas Instruments) invents the germanium
  hybrid IC — the first working integrated circuit.
- **1959:** Robert Noyce (Fairchild Semiconductor, later Intel)
  invents the silicon monolithic IC — the form dominant today.
  [[Gordon Moore]] was at Fairchild at this time.

## Scaling Trajectory (Moore's Law in Practice)
- 1971: Intel 4004 — 2,300 transistors
- 1989: Intel 486 — 1.2 million transistors
- 2012: NVIDIA GTX 680 — ~3.5 billion transistors (runs [[AlexNet]])
- 2025: NVIDIA GB202 — ~92.2 billion transistors (runs LLMs)

This ~40M× increase in 54 years is [[Moore's Law]] at work.

## Impact on AI
IC scaling provided the raw compute that enabled every
generation of AI:
- Early computers (1950s): first AI programs ([[Logic Theorist]])
- Microprocessors (1970s–80s): expert systems
- GPUs (2010s): [[Deep Learning]] via [[GPU Computing]]
- Modern AI chips: [[Large Language Models]] and [[Transformer Architecture]]
