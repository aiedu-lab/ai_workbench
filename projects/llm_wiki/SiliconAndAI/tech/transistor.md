# Transistor

A semiconductor device that amplifies or switches electronic
signals. The transistor is the fundamental unit counted by
[[Moore's Law]] and the building block of all modern computing.

## History
- **1947:** First point-contact transistor (Bardeen, Brattain,
  Shockley at Bell Labs) — Nobel Prize in Physics 1956.
- **1958–59:** [[Integrated Circuit]] combines multiple
  transistors on a single chip.
- **1963:** CMOS process (Sah and Wanlass, Fairchild) — enables
  low-power digital logic.
- **1967:** [[Robert Dennard]] invents DRAM using one transistor
  per bit — enabling affordable computer memory.
- **1974:** [[Robert Dennard]] formulates [[Dennard Scaling]].
- **2012:** FinFET (3D gate) adopted by Intel at 22nm to combat
  leakage — extending [[Moore's Law]] past planar limits.

## Scaling and the AI Connection
Transistor shrinkage from ~10 µm (1970) to ~2 nm (2024) is
what powered all compute growth enabling AI:
- More transistors per chip → faster CPUs → symbolic AI programs
- Billions of transistors per GPU → [[Deep Learning]] becomes practical
- [[AlexNet]] (2012): trained on GPUs with ~3B transistors each
- GPT-4-class training: GPUs with 80B+ transistors

## Physical Limits
Quantum tunneling and heat at atomic scale are slowing
traditional 2D scaling. Industry is exploring FinFETs,
GAAFETs (gate-all-around), 3D stacking, and alternative
materials (InGaAs, graphene).
