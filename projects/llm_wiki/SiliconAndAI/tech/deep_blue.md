# Deep Blue

IBM's chess-playing computer that, on May 11, 1997, became the
first computer to defeat a reigning world chess champion (Garry
Kasparov) under standard tournament conditions.

## Technical Specs
Deep Blue was a custom-built parallel supercomputer using
480 special-purpose chess chips, capable of evaluating ~200
million positions per second.

## The Moore's Law Milestone
The History of AI article explicitly frames Deep Blue as a
[[Moore's Law]] milestone:
- The 1951 Ferranti Mark 1 ran the first chess program.
- Deep Blue's hardware was **10 million times faster** than
  the Ferranti Mark 1.
- This represents ~46 years of [[Moore's Law]] doubling
  (roughly 23 doublings × 2× each ≈ 8M× increase).

Deep Blue was not primarily an AI algorithmic breakthrough —
it won largely through brute-force hardware superiority enabled
by semiconductor scaling.

## Contrast with Later AI
Deep Blue used exhaustive search + expert heuristics
([[Symbolic AI]] paradigm) rather than [[Machine Learning]].
AlphaGo (2016) and AlphaZero (2017) later achieved superhuman
Go performance using [[Deep Learning]] and [[Reinforcement
Learning]] — a fundamentally different approach.
