# AlexNet (2012)

A deep convolutional [[Neural Networks|neural network]] developed
by Alex Krizhevsky under [[Geoffrey Hinton]] at the University
of Toronto. Won the 2012 ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition
Challenge (ILSVRC) with an error rate dramatically lower than
all competitors — triggering a field-wide shift to [[Deep Learning]].

## Why AlexNet Was a Turning Point
1. **Hardware enabler:** Trained on two NVIDIA GTX 580 GPUs
   (~3B transistors each). [[GPU Computing]] provided the
   parallel compute that made training practical. Without this,
   training would have taken months rather than days.
2. **Scale validation:** Confirmed that bigger networks + more
   data + more compute = better AI. Tied AI progress directly
   to [[Moore's Law]]-driven hardware.
3. **Field shift:** Within three years, nearly all image
   recognition approaches abandoned alternatives for deep
   convolutional networks. [[Yann LeCun]]'s earlier CNN work
   (LeNet, 1990) was vindicated.

## The Silicon-AI Feedback Loop
AlexNet's success is the clearest demonstration:

[[Moore's Law]] → GPU transistor scaling →
GPU training speed → AlexNet victory →
[[Deep Learning]] era → massive GPU demand →
NVIDIA becomes world's most valuable company (2024)

## Path to Language
AlexNet used CNNs for vision. The [[Transformer Architecture]]
(2017) generalized deep learning to language, ultimately
producing [[Large Language Models]] like GPT and Claude.
