# AI Winter

Periods of significantly reduced funding and interest in AI
research, caused by overpromising results that hardware and
algorithms could not yet deliver. Two main winters occurred:
1974–1980 (First) and late 1980s – early 1990s (Second).

## First AI Winter (1974–1980)
Triggered by the 1973 Lighthill Report (UK) and DARPA funding
cuts (US):
- Root cause: insufficient computer power. [[Hans Moravec]]
  argued in 1976 that computers were "millions of times too
  weak for useful AI."
- [[Marvin Minsky]] and Seymour Papert's 1969 *Perceptrons*
  book halted [[Neural Networks]] funding for ~10 years.
- DARPA cut $3M/year speech research grant to CMU.

## Second AI Winter (Late 1980s–Early 1990s)
Triggered by the collapse of the expert systems market:
- 1987: Specialized LISP machine market demolished overnight
  when desktop computers (following [[Moore's Law]]) surpassed
  them in price/performance.
- Expert systems proved brittle, expensive to maintain, and
  unable to learn.
- Strategic Computing Initiative cut AI funding "deeply."

## Connection to Silicon Scaling
Both AI winters were fundamentally hardware-constrained events:
- Researchers made promises that required more compute than
  [[Moore's Law]] had yet delivered.
- Both winters ended when hardware scaling caught up:
  the late 1990s brought sufficient compute for machine
  learning; the 2000s–2010s brought GPU compute for
  [[Deep Learning]].

## The AI Boom (2020s)
The current AI era began with the [[Transformer Architecture]]
(2017) and the scaling of [[Large Language Models]]. Unlike
prior cycles, transformer capability scales predictably with
compute — directly tying AI progress to [[Moore's Law]]-driven
hardware.

## Related
- [[Moore's Law]] — hardware growth that eventually ended each winter
- [[Dennard Scaling]] — when power scaling ended, new constraints arose
- [[Symbolic AI]] — dominant paradigm during both winters
- [[Neural Networks]] — suppressed in first winter; revived in second
- [[Deep Learning]] — ended the relevance of AI winters after 2012
- [[Marvin Minsky]] — *Perceptrons* contributed to first winter
- [[Geoffrey Hinton]] — revived neural networks in the 1980s
- [[Expert Systems]] — dominated and then failed in second winter
