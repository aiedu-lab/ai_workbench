# Deep Learning

A class of [[Machine Learning]] methods using multi-layer
[[Neural Networks]] to learn hierarchical representations
directly from raw data. Deep learning proved transformative for
vision, speech, and language tasks after the 2012 breakthrough.

## Key Milestones
- 1943: [[Walter Pitts]] & [[Warren McCulloch]] — idealized neurons
- 1969: [[Marvin Minsky]] suppresses connectionism (*Perceptrons*)
- 1986: [[Geoffrey Hinton]] & Rumelhart — [[Backpropagation]]
  popularized; multi-layer training becomes feasible
- 1990: [[Yann LeCun]] — CNNs read zip codes and checks
- 2012: [[AlexNet]] wins ImageNet; triggers field-wide adoption
- 2017: [[Transformer Architecture]] replaces CNNs for language
- 2020+: [[Large Language Models]] (GPT-3, Claude) dominate

## The Hardware-AI Feedback Loop
Deep learning's story is inseparable from hardware scaling:
1. [[Moore's Law]] doubled compute every 2 years
2. [[Dennard Scaling]] added free efficiency gains until ~2005
3. Its end drove GPU parallelism via [[GPU Computing]]
4. GPU parallel compute enabled training of billion-parameter
   networks — [[AlexNet]] (2012) used two consumer GPUs
5. Larger models validated "scale is all you need" — drove
   massive GPU demand and NVIDIA's $3T+ valuation (2024)

## Why Hardware Was the Bottleneck
[[Geoffrey Hinton]] acknowledged that in the 1980s–90s "our
labeled datasets were thousands of times too small and our
computers were millions of times too slow." The algorithm
([[Backpropagation]]) was correct; the hardware (following
[[Moore's Law]]) needed 20 more years to catch up.
