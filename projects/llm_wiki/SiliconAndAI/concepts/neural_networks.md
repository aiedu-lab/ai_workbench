# Neural Networks

Computational models loosely inspired by biological neurons.
A neural network consists of layers of interconnected nodes
(neurons) that transform inputs through learned weights.

## Early History
- 1943: [[Walter Pitts]] and [[Warren McCulloch]] describe
  idealized artificial neurons showing how logic functions
  can be computed by neural circuits.
- 1951: [[Marvin Minsky]] and Dean Edmonds build SNARC —
  the first neural network machine.
- 1958: [[Frank Rosenblatt]] introduces the [[Perceptron]] —
  a single-layer network with a learning rule.

## The First Suppression (1969)
[[Marvin Minsky]] and Seymour Papert's book *Perceptrons* (1969)
demonstrated fundamental limits of single-layer networks. This
effectively froze connectionist research funding for ~10 years,
contributing to the first [[AI Winter]].

## Revival (1980s)
- [[Geoffrey Hinton]] and David Rumelhart popularized
  [[Backpropagation]] (1986), enabling multi-layer training.
- John Hopfield proved convergence properties of recurrent nets.
- [[Yann LeCun]] applied convolutional networks to digit
  recognition (1990) — the first genuinely useful neural net app.

## Deep Learning Era
Multi-layer networks ([[Deep Learning]]) became dominant after
the 2012 [[AlexNet]] breakthrough. The key enablers were:
1. Large labeled datasets (ImageNet, 2009)
2. GPU compute following [[Moore's Law]]
3. Algorithmic improvements ([[Backpropagation]], dropout, ReLU)

## Hardware Dependency
[[Geoffrey Hinton]] noted that 1980s–90s neural networks were
impractical because "our datasets were thousands of times too
small and our computers millions of times too slow." Moore's Law
fixed the hardware constraint over the following two decades.
