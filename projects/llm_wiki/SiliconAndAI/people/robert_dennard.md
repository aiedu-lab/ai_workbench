# Robert Dennard (1932–2024)

IBM Fellow and electrical engineer. Inventor of DRAM and
formulator of [[Dennard Scaling]].

## Key Contributions
- **DRAM (1967):** Invented dynamic random-access memory —
  enabling affordable, high-density computer memory. DRAM is
  the primary working memory in all modern computers.
- **Dennard Scaling (1974):** Formulated the power-density
  rule: as transistors shrink, power per unit area stays
  constant, so each generation gains speed and efficiency
  simultaneously. Recognized by [[Gordon Moore]] as a key
  corollary to [[Moore's Law]].

## Why Dennard's End Matters for AI
The breakdown of [[Dennard Scaling]] around 2005–2010 forced
chip designers toward massive parallelism rather than faster
single cores. This shift toward [[GPU Computing]] — thousands
of smaller cores running in parallel — aligned perfectly with
[[Deep Learning]] matrix operations.

Without the end of Dennard Scaling pushing chip architects
toward GPU parallelism, the 2012 [[AlexNet]] breakthrough and
the subsequent [[Deep Learning]] revolution would not have
happened on that timeline.
