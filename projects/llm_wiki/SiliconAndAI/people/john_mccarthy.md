# John McCarthy (1927–2011)

American computer scientist who coined the term "Artificial
Intelligence" and made foundational contributions to AI.

## Key Contributions
- **Dartmouth Workshop (1956):** Co-organized with
  [[Marvin Minsky]], [[Claude Shannon]], and Nathan Rochester.
  McCarthy coined the term "Artificial Intelligence" here —
  the founding event of the discipline.
- **Lisp (1958):** Created the Lisp programming language;
  the primary AI research language for 30+ years.
- **Stanford AI Lab (1963):** Founded with a $3M DARPA grant;
  one of the four main AI research centers through the 1970s.
- **Advice Taker (1958):** Early proposal for logic-based AI,
  launching the [[Symbolic AI]] paradigm.

## Hardware Philosophy
McCarthy recognized that hardware limitations were temporary.
His approach — build logically correct systems and rely on
[[Moore's Law]] to eventually deliver the needed compute — was
fundamentally correct, though symbolic AI ultimately proved
insufficient for perception and learning tasks regardless of
hardware.
