# Marvin Minsky (1927–2016)

American cognitive scientist and co-founder of MIT's AI
laboratory. One of the most influential — and sometimes
controversial — figures in AI history.

## Key Contributions
- **SNARC (1951):** Co-built with Dean Edmonds the first
  neural network machine (40 Hebb synapses) as a graduate
  student at Princeton.
- **Dartmouth Workshop (1956):** Co-organized with
  [[John McCarthy]], [[Claude Shannon]], and Nathan Rochester —
  the founding event of AI as a formal discipline.
- **MIT AI Lab:** Co-founded with [[John McCarthy]] in 1959.
- **Perceptrons (1969):** Co-authored with Seymour Papert;
  showed that single-layer [[Neural Networks]] cannot solve
  non-linearly separable problems. This froze connectionist
  research funding for ~10 years, contributing to the first
  [[AI Winter]].
- **Frames (1975):** Proposed "frame" knowledge structures for
  representing common-sense knowledge; influenced object-
  oriented programming.

## The Perceptrons Paradox
Minsky's *Perceptrons* critique was technically correct for
single-layer networks — but the fix (multi-layer networks
trained with [[Backpropagation]]) was unavailable until
[[Geoffrey Hinton]]'s 1986 work, which required hardware
(following [[Moore's Law]]) that did not yet exist.

Minsky's 1967 prediction — "within a generation the problem
of creating AI will be substantially solved" — was wrong in
timeline precisely because 1960s hardware was insufficient.
