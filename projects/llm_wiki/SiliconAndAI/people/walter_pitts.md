# Walter Pitts (1923–1969)

Self-taught American logician and neuroscientist. Co-authored with
[[Warren McCulloch]] the 1943 paper establishing the theoretical
basis for [[Neural Networks]].

## Contribution
The McCulloch-Pitts paper "A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent
in Nervous Activity" (1943) modelled neurons as threshold logic gates.
It demonstrated that a network of such gates could compute any
boolean function — connecting neuroscience, logic, and computation
in a single framework. The paper influenced:
- [[Frank Rosenblatt]] → [[Perceptron]] (1958)
- [[Marvin Minsky]] → SNARC neural net machine (1951)
- The entire [[Neural Networks]] research tradition

## Historical Significance
Pitts worked without modern hardware — his contribution was
theoretical. The gap between the 1943 paper and practical
[[Deep Learning]] is largely a story of [[Moore's Law]]: it took
~70 years of transistor scaling to run the ideas at meaningful scale.
