# Claude Shannon (1916–2001)

American mathematician and electrical engineer at Bell Labs and MIT.
Father of information theory. Co-organized the 1956 Dartmouth Workshop
that founded AI as a discipline.

## Contributions
- **Information theory (1948):** *A Mathematical Theory of Communication*
  defined entropy, channel capacity, and the bit — the foundation of
  all digital communication and storage. Showed that any information
  can be represented as binary digits.
- **Shannon's chess program (1950):** Described the first algorithm
  for computer chess — a direct precursor to [[Deep Blue]]'s search
  methods 47 years later. Estimated a chess-playing computer would
  need ~10¹² operations per game.
- **Dartmouth Workshop (1956):** Co-organizer (with [[John McCarthy]],
  [[Marvin Minsky]], and Nathan Rochester) of the workshop that coined
  "artificial intelligence."

## Silicon Connection
Shannon's information theory is the theoretical reason bit density
matters: more transistors per chip (via [[Moore's Law]]) directly
translates to more information processed per second. His 1950 chess
estimate — that a computer would need tremendous compute — was
validated when [[Deep Blue]] finally won in 1997, requiring 480
custom chips evaluating 200M positions/second.
