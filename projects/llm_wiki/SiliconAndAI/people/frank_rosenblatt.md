# Frank Rosenblatt (1928–1971)

American psychologist and computer scientist at Cornell University.
Inventor of the [[Perceptron]] (1958) — the first machine that could
learn from examples by adjusting connection weights.

## Contributions
- **[[Perceptron]] (1958):** Implemented on an IBM 704, the Mark I
  Perceptron was a learning machine for image classification. Rosenblatt
  predicted it would "learn, make decisions, and translate languages."
- Inspired by [[Walter Pitts]] and [[Warren McCulloch]]'s 1943 paper
  on idealized neurons.

## Legacy
The perceptron's limitations (single-layer, cannot solve XOR) were
exposed by [[Marvin Minsky]] and Papert in *Perceptrons* (1969),
triggering the first [[AI Winter]]. The architecture was rehabilitated
by [[Geoffrey Hinton]]'s [[Backpropagation]] work in 1986.
