# Allen Newell (1927–1992)

Computer scientist and cognitive psychologist. Co-created the
[[Logic Theorist]] and General Problem Solver with [[Herbert Simon]],
establishing [[Symbolic AI]] as a discipline. Turing Award 1975
(shared with Simon).

## Contributions
- **[[Logic Theorist]] (1955–56):** Co-created with [[Herbert Simon]]
  and J. C. Shaw. The first AI program — proved mathematical theorems
  by heuristic search, demonstrating symbolic reasoning on hardware.
- **General Problem Solver (GPS, 1957):** A universal problem-solving
  architecture using means-ends analysis. Influenced all subsequent
  planning systems in [[Symbolic AI]].
- **Soar (1983–):** A cognitive architecture (with John Laird and Paul
  Rosenbloom) attempting to model all of human cognition in a
  unified system. Still actively developed.
- **Unified Theories of Cognition (1990):** Book articulating the
  vision that a single computational model could explain mind.

## Turing Award (1975)
Shared with [[Herbert Simon]] "for basic contributions to artificial
intelligence and the psychology of human cognition."

## Hardware Awareness
Newell understood the connection between hardware capability and AI
ambition. He and Simon ran the Logic Theorist on RAND's JOHNNIAC
computer. In the 1980s, Newell foresaw that commodity hardware
following [[Moore's Law]] would eventually outpace specialized
Lisp machines — a prediction that came true by 1987 (AI Winter
marker: Lisp machine market collapse).
